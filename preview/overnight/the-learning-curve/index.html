An early chapter

Written at 26.
Still part of the work.

I wrote The Learning Curve when I was 26. It is part of the story behind my work in education, culture, and equity.

The book offers educators ways to engage culturally and linguistically diverse students while challenging the school-to-prison pipeline. It asks how schools can build a cultural framework that supports students inside and outside the classroom.

Explore my current research below to see how questions of identity, belonging, and educational justice continue through Black & Bilingual.

Educator · Author · Curriculum creator

The practice behind the pages.

I bring more than a decade of experience in education, including K–12 general and special education, literacy intervention, restorative classrooms, and instruction in a juvenile justice setting.

My educational experience spans carceral, alternative, and special education in Washington, DC, and Metro Atlanta, including Atlanta Public Schools, DeKalb County Schools, and Gwinnett County Schools.

My approach brings culturally responsive teaching, individualized support, and creative curriculum design together. Through Black & Bilingual, I turn that experience into learning materials that connect language, identity, and global awareness.

01

Instruction that adapts

Across Gwinnett County Public Schools and Tapestry Public Charter School, my work has included differentiated lessons, IEP implementation, and small-group support for literacy, executive functioning, and individual learning goals.

02

Literacy & language

As a reading coach at Carver STEAM Academy, I delivered interventions in comprehension and fluency. I have also co-taught fourth and fifth grade English language arts and taught foundational Spanish to young learners.

03

Restorative classrooms

At L.J. Price Middle School, I led a sixth-grade restorative classroom, building relationships and supporting emotional regulation alongside academic learning for students with varied behavioral and learning needs.

04

Learning beyond barriers

At Maya Angelou Academy, I taught career preparation to young people ages 14–20 in a juvenile justice setting and led weekly Harambee meetings supporting social and emotional learning.
