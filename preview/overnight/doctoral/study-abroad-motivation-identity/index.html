Spanish: Study Abroad, Motivation & Identity

An analysis of three forces that can deepen second language acquisition: authentic immersion, learner motivation, and identity.

← Back to Doctoral Coursework & Research Projects

Project overview

This project examines study abroad, motivation, and identity as connected dimensions of Spanish acquisition. Each section moves from research summary to personal example, analysis, and implications for teaching, linking SLA theory to lived experience and classroom practice.

Language learning is not just about doing grammar drills and memorizing vocabulary. Second language acquisition requires connection.

At a glance

Study abroad

Authentic environments require learners to negotiate form, meaning, and use through everyday interaction.

Motivation

Purpose, participation, collaboration, and meaningful learning sustain effort and risk-taking.

Identity

Representation and belonging shape whether learners can see themselves inside the language they are learning.

Teaching transfer

Language cafés, travel simulations, cultural centers, project-based learning, and diverse representation can bring these principles into classrooms.

Lived experience as evidence

I connect SLA theory to my experience in Cojímar, Cuba, where I intentionally relied on Spanish with my host, taxi driver, people in the town square, and even a doctor after an ankle injury. That immersion moved language use away from scripted classroom exchanges and toward real-time listening, adaptation, and confidence.

Identity and the Black diaspora

The identity section traces my Spanish-learning motivation back to Celia Cruz and the realization that Spanish belongs to a racially and culturally diverse world. That perspective influenced travel to Cuba, Colombia, Puerto Rico, and the Dominican Republic and directly informed the creation of Black & Bilingual.

Teaching implications

The project recommends designing for meaningful use: group projects, gamified practice, off-site learning, simulated travel, museums and cultural centers, and exposure to Afro-Latinx, Indigenous, Muslim, LGBTQIA+, and other diverse Spanish-speaking communities.
