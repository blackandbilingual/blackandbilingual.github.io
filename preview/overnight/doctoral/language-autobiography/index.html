Project overview

This short autobiographical project traces the people and language experiences that shaped my linguistic identity. It begins with my grandparents, Callie and Richard Chatman, who spoke African American Vernacular English, moves to my high-school introduction to Celia Cruz and Spanish, and ends with the way AAVE, English, Spanish, storytelling, and diaspora converged in Black & Bilingual.

AAVE, English, and Spanish led me to create Black & Bilingual.

At a glance

Family language history

My grandparents’ AAVE is part of the linguistic history I carry into my work.

Catalyst

A high-school project on Celia Cruz pushed me to take Spanish seriously and opened a wider interest in Afro-Latinx identity.

Contribution

Black & Bilingual became a place to connect language learning with Black diasporic identity and storytelling.

Growth edge

The project identifies SLA, curriculum development, field knowledge, terminology, and professional networks as areas for continued growth.

Why this project matters

This project makes the personal foundation of my doctoral work visible. My interest in bilingualism is not separate from Black language or identity; it grew through them. The autobiography positions language as inheritance, inspiration, identity, and eventually professional practice.

Connection to Black & Bilingual

The final slide names Black & Bilingual as my contribution: a project shaped by AAVE, English, Spanish, storytelling, and the Black diaspora. That connection becomes a throughline in later coursework on second language acquisition, bilingual education, and linguistic justice.
