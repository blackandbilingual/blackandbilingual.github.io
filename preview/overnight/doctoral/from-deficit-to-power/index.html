From Deficit to Power: The Resistant Capital Challenge

Professional development for educators of multilingual students focused on identifying and reframing deficit-based narratives.

Project overview

This project designs a 90-minute professional-development session that moves educators from reflection to theory, collaborative analysis, and concrete instructional action. Its inquiry asks how educators can identify and reframe deficit-based narratives that harm multilingual and culturally diverse students.

By embracing an asset-based lens, educators are empowered to make a difference.

At a glance

Situative learning

Borko’s framework grounds professional learning in reflection, collaboration, authentic practice, and shared inquiry.

Resistant capital

Acevedo & Solórzano extend Community Cultural Wealth by centering advocacy, resilience, and resistance to systemic inequity.

Community Cultural Wealth

The session introduces Yosso’s forms of capital as assets traditional school systems often overlook.

Action orientation

Participants leave by committing to one equity-centered shift they can make in daily practice.

Session design

The 90-minute sequence includes an opening reflection on resistance, a framework overview, a two-part small-group reframing activity, group synthesis, and a closing commitment to practice. The design intentionally moves from personal experience to theory and then to application.

From narrative to asset

In the central activity, educators analyze classroom scenarios, identify the deficit assumption embedded in each one, identify forms of cultural wealth that are already present, and rewrite the narrative using asset-based language. The goal is not just awareness—it is a change in how educators interpret students and families.
