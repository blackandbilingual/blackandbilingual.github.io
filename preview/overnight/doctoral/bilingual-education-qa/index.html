Project overview

This project answers two questions I frequently encounter in my work: What is bilingual education? and Why would families choose it? It translates course research into language that can support conversations with parents, educators, and community members.

The key distinction is whether a program adds to a child’s linguistic identity or gradually replaces it.

What is bilingual education?

Bilingual education uses at least two languages as media of instruction. Programs differ according to learner populations, community contexts, and educational goals.

Additive bilingualism

Strong programs develop another language while maintaining and valuing the home language.

Subtractive bilingualism

Weak programs move students toward English by replacing rather than building on the home language.

Many program forms

Models range from heritage-language maintenance to immersion and two-way bilingual programs.

Community context

Bilingual education adapts to the linguistic and cultural realities of the communities it serves.

Why families choose bilingual programs

Academic strength

Home-language development can support broader academic learning, including English.

Long-term opportunity

Bilingualism and biliteracy can expand educational, professional, and communication opportunities.

Identity & community

Language connects children to family, cultural roots, and community belonging.

Global advantage

Communicating in more than one language expands participation across communities and contexts.

Connection to Black & Bilingual

I selected these questions because they come up regularly in conversations around Black & Bilingual. Being able to explain bilingual education clearly strengthens the educational foundation behind my books, products, and community-facing work.
