From Code to Connection

How AAVE speakers may acquire Romance languages through identity, linguistic transfer, and cultural resonance.

Project overview

This research proposal challenges second language acquisition frameworks that treat Standard English as the default baseline. It centers heritage AAVE speakers learning Spanish, French, and Portuguese and asks how dialect awareness, identity, and global Black language connections may shape acquisition.

AAVE is positioned not as a barrier, but as a bridge to multilingual competence.

At a glance

Research question 1

How do heritage AAVE speakers experience Romance-language learning through identity and dialect awareness?

Research question 2

How might exposure to global Black Romance-language varieties influence motivation, confidence, and engagement?

Global connections

The proposal considers Palenquero Spanish, Haitian Creole, and Cape Verdean Portuguese as examples of Afro-diasporic linguistic connection.

Framework

SLA research is brought together with AAVE scholarship, culturally sustaining pedagogy, linguistic justice, and work on pidgins and creoles.

Research design

The proposed study uses an exploratory qualitative design with 6–8 heritage AAVE-speaking college students enrolled in intermediate language courses at Florida A&M University, Morehouse College, Howard University, and Winston-Salem State University. Data would come from interviews/storytelling, reflection journals, and classroom observations.

Research goals

  • Investigate identity-driven language acquisition and dialect awareness.
  • Explore linguistic patterns between AAVE and Romance languages, including global Black varieties.
  • Develop pedagogical recommendations that affirm AAVE as an asset in second language acquisition.

Intended contribution

The proposal aims toward an equity-based framework for SLA that values global Black language communities and broadens who is centered in language-learning research, curriculum design, and policy conversations.
